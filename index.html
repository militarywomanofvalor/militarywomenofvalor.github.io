Who We Are

Dedicated to the women who served

Military Women of Valor is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit organization dedicated to honoring, supporting and uplifting our nation's female veterans. We connect women to safe housing, supportive services and pathways to lasting independence through referrals and partnerships and we advocate for a transition system built around the realities women face when they come home.

This is not about charity. It is about justice, dignity and a system worthy of her service.

Our Vision

A future where every female veteran has access to safe, affordable housing and critical supportive services.

Our Mission

To honor the service and sacrifice of female veterans by providing safe transitional housing options, supportive service referrals and pathways to permanent housing and lasting success.

Our Approach

Enhancing the system, not recreating it

Military Women of Valor is not trying to build a new system. We work hand in hand with federal, state and local partners and with veterans organizations and associations to strengthen what already works and to close the gaps where women are falling through. This is a different way of moving forward in a new era, one built on partnership and on the lived experience of the women who served.

By the Numbers

The women who served and what they face at home

Women are one of the fastest growing groups in the veteran community. The data shows both the strength they bring and the gaps that remain in the systems meant to serve them.

2M+women veterans live in the United States, about 10 percent of all veterans
17.9%of active duty members are women and 22.4 percent of the Selected Reserve
1 in 3women veterans report military sexual trauma when screened by the VA
24%increase in homelessness among women veterans from 2020 to 2023
28%of active duty mothers are single parents, compared with 8 percent of fathers
54%of women veterans did not feel prepared to navigate community resources, compared with 35 percent of men
33%of women veterans found a job within three months of leaving service, compared with 41 percent of men
18%projected share of the veteran population who will be women by 2040, up from 4 percent in 2000

The Transition Gap

The most dangerous year is the first one home

More than 200,000 service members leave the military every year. In the first year after separation, veterans die by suicide at nearly twice the rate of the overall veteran population. Researchers call this window the deadly gap: military health care ends before VA and community care begin.

Women carry added weight

Women leave service more likely to be single parents, more likely to have experienced military sexual trauma and less likely to be recognized as veterans by employers, landlords and even health care providers.

Systems built for another era

Transition programs, housing options and veteran services were designed for a force that looked very different decades ago. Few transitional homes accept children and many shelters are not built for women.

Isolation turns hardship into crisis

Without a connected network, a lost job or an unsafe relationship can become a housing crisis within weeks. Early connection to peers and services is the strongest protection.

A New Infrastructure for Transition

A clear path from uniform to her next chapter

A successful transition starts long before the final day in uniform and continues well after it. This is the path Military Women of Valor encourages every servicewoman, family and community partner to follow.

  1. 12 to 24 months before separation

    Plan

    • Begin the Transition Assistance Program early, no later than one year out
    • Build a budget for at least six months of civilian living costs
    • Map housing, child care and school options in the community you will call home
    • Request copies of your medical and service records
  2. 6 months before separation

    Prepare

    • File a VA disability claim through the Benefits Delivery at Discharge program
    • Explore DoD SkillBridge for civilian training and internships
    • Translate military experience into a civilian resume and professional network
    • Connect with a women veteran peer group before you leave
  3. The first 90 days

    Connect

    • Enroll in VA health care and schedule a women's health appointment
    • Meet a local veteran service officer to review your benefits
    • Confirm stable housing and a safety plan for you and your children
    • Save the Veterans Crisis Line and Women Veterans Call Center numbers
  4. The first year and beyond

    Thrive

    • Use the GI Bill or Veteran Readiness and Employment for education and training
    • Keep regular mental health and peer support in place
    • Build savings, credit and a path toward home ownership
    • Give back as a mentor to the next woman making the transition

What We Advocate For

Six pillars of a system worthy of her service

Safe, child inclusive housing

Transitional and permanent housing that never forces a mother to choose between her children and a roof.

Gender specific health care

Comprehensive women's health and mental health services in every community.

Trauma informed support

Care that acknowledges military sexual trauma and the unique experiences of women in service.

Career and financial readiness

Programs that recognize military skills, open doors to employers and build long term financial stability.

Warm handoffs, not cold exits

A direct connection from military care to VA and community care, so no woman falls through the gap.

Recognition and belonging

Peer networks and public recognition that affirm every woman who served is a veteran.

Mission Possible

It takes an army. Together this mission is possible.

Every woman we serve once raised her hand to serve our nation. Today many carry the weight of trauma, financial hardship and the loss of a home, often in silence. Your gift and your time tell her that she is seen, that she is valued and that she is not alone.

Your mission, if you choose to accept it, is to stand with her. It will be rewarding, gratifying and life changing, for her and for you.
